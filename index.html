doggy wanna eat

doggy wanna eat is a 2D platformer about a very hungry dog. Each level, doggy craves certain foods, and he has to eat all of them before they go rotten. Eat something rotten and it's game over.

my contributions

Details. Solo project, currently in progress. Made in Unity 6 with C#. For my third year at DigiPen, my group and I are working on building an application on Unity 6, so I wanted to practice coding in C# and get used to the interface. I drew all the art myself in MS Paint. The project is public on GitHub.

Movement. Doggy moves with physics on a Rigidbody2D, updated in FixedUpdate so his speed stays consistent. He can only jump while touching ground, checked with a layer mask, and his fall speed is capped. If he walks or falls off the edge of the screen, he wraps around and comes back in on the opposite side.

Freshness. Every piece of food runs its own freshness timer, shown as a circular clock above it that drains and fades from green to red. When the clock runs out, the food swaps to a rotten sprite. Each food type is its own prefab with its own freshness time, so new foods can be added without touching the code.

Cravings. At the start of each level, a thought bubble pops up over doggy's head one bubble at a time, showing what he craves and how many (e.g. apple x2). The cravings are set per level in the Inspector. The bubble follows doggy as he moves, counts down as he eats, and finishes the level once everything is eaten. Players can press X to open and close it.

Penny the cat. Penny hisses and turns angry when doggy gets close. If he gets too close, the two disappear into a dust cloud and fight for five seconds while the food keeps rotting. Penny's hiss and fight ranges are drawn in the editor so they're easy to tune.

Levels & UI. I built a tutorial level with on-screen prompts, a bad ending and a level complete screen that pause the game, and replay, next level and home buttons that load scenes. The buttons squish when hovered, using unscaled time so they still animate while the game is paused.

Carthur and the Riddle Curse

Carthur and the Riddle Curse is a 2D puzzle platformer wherein Carthur, the Chosen One, must defeat the riddle-loving, dastardly evil King Meowdred.

my contributions

Details. First year, 5-person group, all programmers. Written in C++ using CProcessing.

Level 2: the time-travel lever puzzle. I programmed the game's second level, built around three statue levers that switch the room between the present, past and future. Pulling a lever swaps in a different set of platforms, an exit door in a new spot, and a different group of Shadow Cat enemies, all loaded from text files so the layouts could be changed without touching the code. Only one lever can be active at a time, and pulling it again resets the room. The player has to solve King Meowdred's riddle to work out which era is the right one. The correct door leads to Level 3, while a wrong door sends them back to the start of the level.

Riddles and doors. I built the riddle scroll that players can click to open and close at any time, showing the riddle on a parchment background signed by Meowdred. I also wrote the shared door code (drawing the doors and detecting when Carthur walks through one), which is used across the game's levels.

Story & Art. I pitched the idea of using the myth of King Arthur as a main reference as my group was fond of a medieval concept. My team decided on the types of levels we wanted and I made the riddles after. I also wrote the narration for the cutscene and Meowlin and Meowdred's dialogue. Two other teammates and I worked on the assets. I sketched concepts and with my team's feedback, completed the final designs in pixel art using Piskel.

Cupid's Arrow

Cupid's Arrow is a 2D society building game. Build your unique society! Play Cupid and aim with your arrow to match characters and watch them shape the world.

my contributions

Details. First year, second half. 5-person group, all programmers. Written in C++ using DigiPen's Alpha Engine.

Aiming and shooting. I programmed some of the arrow. Players aim the arrow with A and D. I added angle compensation so sideways shots still reach their targets, and missed shots that fly off-screen cost the player 3 seconds on the countdown timer, which I also built.

Matchmaking. I wrote the townsfolk (NPCs), who walk back and forth in rows at random speeds, in two types: smart and normal. Hitting one makes a heart appear over their head, and hitting a second forms a couple. Smart + smart, normal + normal and smart + normal each count as a different kind of match. If a struck NPC walks off-screen before being matched, the match is lost. Matched couples disappear with a celebration pop-up and respawn a few seconds later.

Growing the society. Every match shapes the town. As each kind of couple reaches 3, 4 and 5 matches, new buildings appear in the background: futuristic towers for smart couples, offices for mixed couples and houses for normal couples. Normal + normal couples add two new people to the crowd, normal + smart adds one, smart + smart does not add any.

Art. The game was based off old matchmaking Flash games on y5 and y8. One teammate and I worked on the art. I drew the characters, buildings, tutorial images, all endings and the heart animation that appears when a match is made.

Rock & Ghoul

Rock & Ghoul is a 2.5D action game where the player fights spirits using a guitar, which acts as both a melee weapon and spellcasting tool. Rock the stage, strike the arrows, and unleash music as magic.

my contributions

Details. Second year, 8-person group, 6 programmers, 1 designer and 1 artist. Made in C++ in custom engine my group made, uses FMOD for audio.

Audio engine. I built the game's audio system on top of FMOD. Every sound is listed in a JSON manifest with unique names. Sounds only load when they're first needed, and related sounds are grouped into banks that load and unload together for each level, keeping memory use down.

Music. Each scene can have its own background track plus an optional ambience layer underneath, like room tone or crowd applause. When the scene changes, the music fades out with the screen transition and the next track starts once it finishes. Pausing the game muffles the music with a low-pass filter instead of cutting it off, and the player's saved volume setting is applied when the game starts.

Sound effects and spatial audio. I set up 2D spatial audio, so sounds like elemental attacks and footsteps get quieter the further they are from the camera. Actions like guitar strums, plucks, grunts and voice lines have several variations each, so repeated actions don't sound the same. Gameplay sounds are blocked while paused, but menu and button sounds still play. I also hooked sounds into gameplay across the game, from attacks and enemy hits to UI buttons.

Editor tools. I made an audio component for our engine's entity system, so teammates could attach sounds to objects in the level editor, picking them from a dropdown and adjusting volume and spatial settings.

Story & dialogue. I pitched this story. My group's artist and designer asked me for story and character documents to build their work off. Those were ungraded and simply for the flavour of the world and characters, and I really enjoyed writing those. I came up with the idea of using a fretboard as part of the UI. I wrote the cutscene dialogue, following Rock's search for Ghoul, her betrayal, and the two of them making up after the final fight. I also sourced for most of the sounds on DigiPen's provided sound libraries; Soundly and Freesound. Some SFX such as correct/wrong note played was custom made by the audio professors. I am also the voice of the main enemy, Ghoul.
